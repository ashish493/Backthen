## Findings

1. **Speedup over the baseline: 1.62× on set A (77.4 → 47.9 s, cold and warm alike) and about 1.7× on set B (540 → 315 s).**
   These are much smaller than the 15.8× on photos alone (`EXP_PIPELINE.md`), because videos dominate wall time in both sets and the storage limits B.
2. **Cache state does not matter on the internal SSD** (A cold 47.9 s vs warm 48.1 s), matching `EXP_COLD.md`.
3. **On the USB hard drive the pipeline is mostly storage-bound.** It reads 52–57 MB/s while CPU averages 48–55% and the GPU 13–17%.
   A rough floor for reading set B alone sequentially, from the measured drive curves (photos 69 MB/s, videos 90 MB/s), is about 230 s; measured wall is 300–335 s, about 75% of that floor.
   Some of the gap is compute that does not overlap with reading: CPU video decode alone needs about 190 s.
4. **Scheduling choices made no measurable difference:** mixed 319.5 s, phased 315.1 s, 4 readers 298.5 s (single run) all sit inside the 303–336 s spread of repeat runs.
   The raw read test found one reader better than several (69 vs 55 MB/s), but the pipeline shows no penalty for 4 readers, so that effect is not visible end to end at this noise level.
5. **Phased scheduling does change how contended the drive is** (not the total): summed video decode time 543 s phased vs 963 s mixed, p95 video latency 10.5 s vs 18.0 s, p95 photo latency 268 vs 399 ms, peak RSS 3.3 vs 4.5 GB.
   In mixed mode the video decoders wait on a drive that is also serving photo reads. Phasing removes that contention but also removes the overlap, so wall time is equal.
6. **The GPU is not a factor.** It was busy 19–23 s out of 300–335 s (about 7%) and waited for input the rest of the time. Embedding is about 1 ms per photo.
7. **No GPU throttling seen:** max temperature 72–73 °C, SM clock reached 1,897–1,912 MHz.
8. **Memory stayed within budget:** peak RSS 3.2–4.5 GB (target ≤ 6 GB), VRAM 1.4–1.5 GB (target ≤ 3.5 GB).
9. **Formats:** HEIC photos (115), HEVC (68) and VP9 (1) videos, and MOV containers all processed without errors.
   VP9 cannot use NVDEC in this pipeline and went to the CPU pool. One video failed on NVDEC and was redone on the CPU deterministically (recorded in the manifest).

## Follow-up tested: one sequential reader for everything, videos included (`--video-prefetch-mb 256`)
The same single reader that reads photos also reads every video up to 256 MB whole into memory; decoders then work from RAM. Output is bit-identical to the default path (0 embedding differences over 170 files on set A).

| set B, cold, optimised | n | wall s (median) | range | disk MB/s | CPU % | peak RSS MiB | video decode (summed worker time) | p95 video latency |
|---|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|--:|
| mixed schedule (videos read by path) | 3 | 329.2 | 302.8–336.3 | 50 | 46 | 4,689 | 963 s | 18.0 s |
| **prefetch (videos read by the photo reader)** | 3 | **286.2** | 277.3–288.7 | **62** | 62 | 4,080 | **443 s** | **9.0 s** |
| baseline (serial) | 1 | 540.2 | – | 35 | 54 | 3,242 | – | – |

- **13% faster than the mixed schedule** (the ranges do not overlap) and **1.89× faster than the baseline** (540 → 286 s).
- Mechanism: decoders no longer wait on a drive that is also serving photo reads (summed decode time 963 → 443 s, p95 video latency halved); the drive now streams at 62 MB/s on average instead of 50.
- The remaining gap to the rough read-only floor (about 230 s) is 24%.
- Memory: peak RSS 4.1 GB (read budget raised to 768 MB), still under the 6 GB target. Videos above 256 MB still use direct reads.

## Caveats
- Baseline runs once each; B optimised variants 1–2 times, so differences under about 10% (mixed vs phased vs 4 readers) are not resolved.
- The baseline is a minimal-parallel configuration of the same program (photo and video workers still run concurrently), not a strictly one-thread run.
- Set B is one person's archive on one drive; other drives (and USB ports) will differ. Subsets were not used for these runs; all files were processed.
- A + B was run once.
- Warm-cache runs are available only for set A (set B is larger than the file cache).
